2.3.2 Rollout for Long Video Generation
Existing methods for long video generation follow two main paradigms. The first turns a video diffusion model into a
causal generator, as in CausVid Yin et al. [2025] and Self Forcing Huang et al. [2026c]. A block-wise causal attention
mask restricts each chunk to attend only to itself and earlier chunks, and the video is generated chunk by chunk with a
KV cache. The second paradigm, adopted by InfiniteTalk Yang et al. [2025] and LongCat-Video Team et al. [2025a],
keeps the model fully bidirectional within each clip and extends the video by repeatedly continuing from the last
frames of the previously generated clip. We refer to this paradigm as autoregressive video continuation: generation is
autoregressive across clips, while each clip is still denoised with full bidirectional attention. LongCat-Video-Avatar
follows the second paradigm.
Formally, let F and C denote the number of video frames in a clip and its overlap context, respectively, and let L and
Lctx denote the corresponding numbers of latent frames after VAE encoding. The stride between clips is S = F − C.
In a rollout of N clips, clip k ∈ {0, . . . , N − 1} covers frames [kS, kS + F ) and is driven by the audio window Ak
over the same interval. Let E and D denote the VAE encoder and decoder, xref the reference frame, and ctext the text
prompt. The first clip is generated in the image-audio-to-video (IA2V) setting, with the reference latent zref = E(xref )
placed at the first temporal position. For k ≥ 1, we decode the generated latents ˆzk−1 of the previous clip to obtain the
video ˆxk−1 = D(ˆzk−1). We jointly re-encode its last C frames to obtain the context latents of clip k.
zctx,k = E(ˆxk−1[F − C : F ]) . (4)
The context latents zctx,k occupy the first Lctx temporal positions of clip k and are concatenated with Gaussian noise
along the temporal axis. The context latents are kept at timestep zero, and only the remaining L − Lctx positions are
denoised. The reference latent zref is additionally provided as an identity condition for continuation clips, so that the
appearance stays anchored to xref . When the clips are assembled, the first clip is kept in full, and for every subsequent
clip only the S newly generated frames are appended.
Each clip is sampled with the eight-step generator described above. Since the procedure has no fixed horizon, it can
produce videos of arbitrary length. However, it also introduces a mismatch between training and inference. The few-step
generator is distilled on individual clips, whose temporal contexts are derived from real videos, whereas during rollout
every context after the first clip comes from the model’s own imperfect outputs. Small deviations in color, texture, or the
lip region are re-encoded into the next context and amplified over successive clips, which appears as progressive color
drift and loss of detail in long videos. The next section addresses this mismatch by training directly on such rollouts.
